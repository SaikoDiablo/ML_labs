# Lab 6 Report — Training Pipelines and Project Proposal

## Goal
The goal of this lab was to turn my best Lab 3 model (CNN on Fashion-MNIST) into a **reproducible, YAML-driven training pipeline** and to study how training-system choices affect performance and efficiency. Specifically, I aimed to: log experiments with MLflow and save resumable checkpoints,  profile one epoch to find where time is spent, measure the impact of `num_workers` and batch size on throughput/memory/accuracy, compare learning-rate schedules and optimizers under an equal 10-epoch budget, verify gradient accumulation (effective batch 256 via 64×4), demonstrate smooth resume from an interrupted run, and prepare a feasible individual project proposal with deployment constraints.

## Method
- **Environment & reproducibility:** I used an isolated Python environment and recorded versions with `src/print_versions.py` (saved to `results/versions.txt`). All runs are driven by a single `config.yaml`. I fixed seeds with `random.seed(42)`, `np.random.seed(42)`, `torch.manual_seed(42)`, and `torch.cuda.manual_seed_all(42)` (when available) and used deterministic cuDNN settings for reproducibility.
- **Dataset & model:** Fashion-MNIST (28×28 grayscale, 10 classes), normalized with mean 0.2860 / std 0.3530, split into 55,000 train / 5,000 validation / 10,000 test using a seeded generator. The baseline model is the Lab 3 **CNN** (two Conv-ReLU-MaxPool blocks followed by a linear classifier), selected as the best accuracy/cost trade-off in Lab 3.
- **Pipeline (Task 1):** `src/train.py` reads `config.yaml`, builds data loaders/model/optimizer/scheduler, trains with validation each epoch, evaluates on the official test set, saves `checkpoint_last`, `checkpoint_best`, and per-epoch checkpoints (including model, optimizer, scheduler state, epoch, and history), and logs params/metrics/artifacts to MLflow.
- **Profiling (Task 2):** I profiled one training epoch with `torch.profiler` (`src/experiments.py profile`) and reported the time breakdown across data loading, forward pass, backward pass, and optimizer step (profiler table + aggregated shares).
- **`num_workers` study (Task 3):** I varied `num_workers` in `[0, 1, 2, 4, 8]`, measured epoch wall-clock/throughput (samples/s), and plotted throughput vs workers to identify the bottleneck.
- **Batch-size study (Task 4):** I varied batch size in `[32, 64, 128, 256, 512]` and recorded throughput (samples/s), peak memory, and final test accuracy after 5 epochs.
- **Schedules & optimizers (Task 5):** Under a 10-epoch budget I compared LR schedules **StepLR, CosineAnnealingLR, ReduceLROnPlateau** and compared **SGD+momentum** vs **Adam**.
- **Gradient accumulation (Task 6):** I compared an actual batch of 256 against an effective batch of 256 implemented as batch 64 with 4 accumulation steps (loss scaled by 1/4, optimizer step every 4 micro-batches).
- **Resume (Task 7):** I trained for 3 epochs, stopped, set `resume_from` to the epoch-3 checkpoint, and continued to 10 epochs to confirm curves continue smoothly (optimizer/scheduler/history restored).
- **Proposal (Task 8):** I prepared a one-page individual project proposal in `project_proposal.md` including problem, dataset, success metric, deployment constraints (latency ≤ 50 ms, size ≤ 5 MB), and planned optimization.
- **Timing convention:** throughput timings were measured as epoch wall-clock/sample rates during the studies on the same machine; runs were repeated across experiments and reported consistently, following the lab’s timing guidance. Device used: **CPU**.

### Task 3 — `num_workers` study
Source: `results/workers_study.json`, `results/workers_bottleneck.png`

| num_workers | Epoch time (s) | Throughput (samples/s) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 26.34 | 2088.2 |
| 1 | 25.22 | 2181.1 |
| 2 | 34.72 | 1584.0 |
| 4 | 52.04 | 1056.9 |
| 8 | 156.82 | 350.7 |

*Plot:* `results/workers_bottleneck.png`  
*Bottleneck/choice:* Throughput peaks at **1 worker** (2181.1 samples/s) and then degrades sharply as workers increase. On this Windows CPU setup, additional workers add process-spawn and inter-process data-transfer overhead that outweighs any loading overlap, so the pipeline becomes limited by worker/IPC overhead rather than by data loading. (PyTorch also warned that 8 workers exceeds the suggested max for this system.)

### Task 7 — Resume from checkpoint
- Interrupted at epoch: **3** (checkpoint: `results/checkpoint_epoch_3.pth`, pre-resume history saved as `results/history_epoch1-3.json`).
- Resumed to epoch: **10** (checkpoint: `results/checkpoint_last.pth`, full history in `results/history.json`).
- Epoch 1–3 (before interrupt): val acc 84.86% → 87.04% → 88.80%, test 88.55%.
- Resumed epoch 4–10: `Resumed from ...checkpoint_epoch_3.pth at epoch 4`, val acc 88.38% → 89.18% → 89.90% → 89.84% → 90.68% → 90.68% → 91.08%, test 90.06%.
- **Observation:** The metrics continue smoothly across the interruption with no reset/jump at the 3→4 boundary, confirming that model, optimizer, scheduler, and history state were correctly restored.

## Conclusions
1. **Pipeline & reproducibility:** Structuring training around a single YAML config with fixed seeds, saved checkpoints (model/optimizer/scheduler/history), and MLflow logging made experiments repeatable and easy to compare. In particular, restoring the full training state allowed an interrupted run to resume at epoch 4 and continue smoothly to epoch 10 (test 90.06%) without restarting or destabilizing the curves.
2. **Bottlenecks & data-loading efficiency:** Profiling showed the CNN training on CPU was compute-bound (forward ~57%, backward ~40%, data loading ~1.5%, optimizer ~1.2%). The `num_workers` study confirmed that adding workers did not help beyond a low setting: throughput peaked at 1 worker (2181.1 samples/s) and fell sharply at 2–8 workers due to Windows process-spawn/IPC overhead, so the practical bottleneck shifted from computation/data access to multiprocessing overhead.
3. **Accuracy–throughput–memory trade-offs:** Larger batches improved throughput up to a point (best around 128 at 1814.8 samples/s) but reduced 5-epoch accuracy at higher sizes (512 → 87.79%). Under a 10-epoch budget, Adam with cosine annealing gave the best result (90.21%), outperforming SGD+momentum (86.24%). Gradient accumulation effectively emulated batch 256 with batch 64×4 (88.86% vs 88.83%), showing a practical way to reach a larger effective batch with similar training behavior.
